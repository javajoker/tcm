# Changelog

All notable changes are recorded here, newest first, in the spirit of [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/). Versions are of the **app**; the release notes of each version also state
the **knowledge-base**, **engine** and **parameters** versions and list safety-relevant changes separately ([release process §3](docs/release-process.md)). Until the first release, entries
are grouped under *Unreleased*.

### Offline use (in progress, Release A)
- **A service worker** (PM-04): after one visit the whole product works without a connection, in Chromium, Safari's engine and Firefox (end-to-end scenario E22 stops the server and runs a whole assessment). Hand-written, about 2 KB gzip, no library: it precaches the files of the build (the knowledge files of the script in use when the page asks), answers navigations to the app with the cached page, and answers nothing else — no request with a body, nothing from another origin, no file outside the build. The build writes it last from the files it has just written, and `check-release` (rule 13) recomputes its lists; the host never caches `/sw.js`; the CSP names `worker-src` and `manifest-src`; a kill worker (`scripts/make-kill-sw.ts`) removes it from every browser that visits, and the development build serves it as its `sw.js`. 
- **Updates, the offline copy in Settings, and a boot guard** (PM-05): a newer build installs beside the running one and waits — "A new version is ready" with *Reload* and *Later*, never applied by itself and never in the middle of an answer; Settings → *Offline use* says the state of the copy in words and removes it (*Erase everything* removes the worker too); without a connection, a switch to the script whose files are not saved yet says it needs a connection once. A tiny script of its own (`/boot.js`) counts starts that never render: after two in a row it drops the offline copy and loads once from the network, so a faulty release cannot trap a person. The rollback is rehearsed with two real builds (scenarios E22b–E22d, in Chromium, Safari's engine and Firefox): deploy B over A, A over B, the draft kept each time, and a build that cannot start removed after two tries. 
- **Installable** (PM-06): the manifest is standalone; the browser's install offer is held back and shown only as an *Install* button in Settings (nothing on a first visit, nothing by itself), and Settings always says in words how to "Add to Home Screen", iPhone and iPad included. `check-release` (rule 14) reads the manifest back: its fields, nothing beyond the icon and the window, each icon present and of the size it says, an Apple touch icon.

### Learn (in progress, Release B)
- **The Learn section** (PM-13): a *Learn* link in the header opens a hub with a search field and a card for each kind of page that exists — for now the glossary (163 terms, each in Chinese, pinyin and English with where the wording comes from) and the classical quotations the app cites (127, by book, with their text and whether the wording was checked against the source). Search finds an entry by its Chinese in either script, its English, its pinyin with or without tone marks, its alternative names and its address, ranked exact, then prefix, then substring, grouped by kind, with a link to the whole filtered list; it works from the knowledge the page already holds, so it adds no download and works offline. Lists are grouped and filterable (the count is announced), pages have one `h1`, a *Sources* section with the review state, related entries and one neutral line at the foot about the self-assessment. A page about something a person might use (the next tasks bring formulas, points and foods) puts its cautions and the record's flags first, open, under a line saying it is general information and not advice for the reader; the catalogue is scanned in a test for second-person wording. Every food and glossary term now has a stable ASCII id that is never changed once published, so addresses survive corrections. In all three languages; end-to-end E26 (Chrome, Safari's engine, Firefox) and the offline scenario cover it. Entry-point change: every screen's header has one more link, so the visual baselines are to be regenerated by the workflow.
- **Pattern and constitution pages** (PM-14): all 23 patterns, listed by group with the direction of care under each name, and the nine constitutions. A pattern page gives the group, the direction of care, the tongue and pulse, the typical features **in bands** (key, common, supporting, and what speaks against it — never raw weights), the smaller patterns it is built from, its sources or a plain "no source", and the other patterns of its group. A constitution page gives the description, the features traditionally associated with the type, the nature words it relates to and the external factors it is said to be more prone to, in words, and says that a type is a tendency and not a label. The treatment lists of a pattern and the questionnaire items of a constitution are left out on purpose: the first describe something a person might use and wait for the pages that put cautions first; the second are statements for a person to answer. The bands come from one function in the engine that the comparison of patterns will reuse.
- **Pages about something a person might use** (PM-15): formulas (33; the 20 tier-A formulas in a release), the 31 acupressure points by WHO code and the 47 foods. Each puts a *Cautions* section first, open, under a line saying the page is general information and not advice for the reader: the record's own cautions, the stored pregnancy flag, every stored interaction (or "none recorded, not yet reviewed"), an allergy line, and for a formula the tier and its reasons and a note that a practitioner sets a formula for the person it is meant for. Then, for a formula, the school and source, the direction of care, the composition as a table with the role of each herb (amounts only where the data has them — a release has none), the reasoning, how far the composition was checked and its sources; for a point, where it lies and how a point is traditionally pressed; for a food, its nature, flavours and functions and why it is listed. A pattern page links to the formulas, points and foods its record lists, only as links. **The build now also refuses a formula whose stored interaction list differs from its herbs'**, as it already did for the pregnancy flag. Twenty-two English strings of the curated data that spoke to the reader were reworded where a page shows them; a test scans every page of every kind in both languages for second-person wording. Also fixed: a strong herb in a formula's tier reason showed in Traditional characters on a Simplified page.
- **Compare patterns** (PM-16): two or three patterns side by side at `/learn/compare?ids=…` — the group, direction of care and tongue and pulse of each; the features they share; the features that tell them apart, as bands (*key*, *common*, *supporting*, *speaks against*, *not in the record*) and never as numbers; and the topics of the assessment's questions that bring out the differences, with the features each asks about (the questions themselves speak to the person, so they are not quoted). Reached from a *Compare with* section on every pattern page, from the hub, and from each *Also possible* pattern of a result. Computed from the records alone by one function in the engine, with no score and nothing about any person; a feature counts as telling patterns apart by the same two-point rule the question bank's check uses. Wide tables scroll inside their own focusable box with the row headers kept in view.

### Trends (in progress, Release B)
- **Trends** (PM-19): History gets a *Results | Trends* tab list once three results were made with the same version of the rules. *Trends* shows the five phases and the cold–heat and deficiency–excess axes as band dot-strips — one mark for each result at its band, the date and the season under it, no line between marks — with a table twin (the band in words and the number beside it) and a link from each mark to its result; what moved between bands in neutral words (*Between 3 Sept and 1 Oct, Earth moved from the somewhat high band to the normal band.*), never a movement inside a band; the symptoms that appeared or are no longer reported; the leading patterns of each result as one row per pattern; a note when pregnancy, breastfeeding or a long-term condition changed between two results; and a fixed reminder that answers vary with sleep, mood, food and the season. ### Follow-up (in progress, Release B)
- **Look again later** (PM-18): a card at the end of a result offers *In 2 weeks, In 4 weeks, In 8 weeks* or *Not now*, nothing pre-selected, and writes a date — the start of that day — on the saved result at once (deleted with it, and carried by a backup). From that day a card on the start page and in History asks *Time to look again?*, only if the person has not dismissed it, has not saved a newer result and is not in the middle of an assessment; there is no timer, no notification and nothing sent. The file is checked against a strict RFC 5545 reader written for the test; the whole path, with the browser's clock moved on, runs in Chrome, Safari's engine and Firefox.

### Lock (in progress, Release B)
- **The local data lock** (PM-20): *Lock the history* in Settings turns on an optional passphrase lock. The saved results and the unfinished assessment are then stored encrypted — a random 256-bit key does the encrypting (AES-256-GCM, a fresh random value for every write), and the passphrase only wraps that key (PBKDF2-SHA-256, 600 000 rounds) — so someone who gets this browser's files, or a copy of them, sees nothing readable; the language, theme and other settings stay readable, because the lock screen needs them. Turning it on asks for a backup first (one from the last quarter of an hour, or a box that says a forgotten passphrase loses the history), then the passphrase twice with a strength hint; every record and the lock are written in one transaction, tested with a database that fails at every step — the store is either as it was or fully locked. The key lives only in memory: *Lock now* in the header, the idle time (5, 10 — the default —, 30 or 60 minutes, also for a laptop that slept) and any reload bring back a lock screen that shows nothing of the history and takes the passphrase, the language and the theme. After five wrong passphrases each next try waits longer, up to five minutes (a screen reader hears the wait once, and when it is over), and the screen says honestly that this slows down a person at the keyboard and not someone with a copy of the data. A passphrase can be changed (one small write, the history is not encrypted again) or the lock turned off. A forgotten passphrase cannot be recovered by anyone — the lock screen offers *Erase everything on this device*, after which a backup made earlier can be restored. A second tab cannot write around the lock, and hears when it changes. A backup made while the lock is on defaults to a passphrase-protected file. The Settings table of what is stored lists the lock record. The lock does not defend against malware or a malicious extension and says so. The end-to-end scenario E30 runs in Chrome (Traditional on a phone, Simplified, English), Safari's engine and Firefox; the security review is a human task that is still to be done, with a checklist in the design. The web package's test timeout is now 20 seconds, because the suite grew to more than 1,200 tests that share the machine with the other packages'.

### Library expansion (in progress, Release C)
- **Admission tooling** (PM-21): the checklist a pattern must pass to enter the library now runs in the build. Every row a machine can check is a function (`scripts/kb/admission.py`, run by the knowledge-base validator): identity and names, a verified quotation and a textbook source, the evidence table, that every weighted symptom can be asked, that the typical patient ranks first with 20 points over every other pattern — or a recorded exception — and that **every** pair closer than that has three separating questions (the earlier test named three pairs), tongue and pulse, a tier-A formula or a declaration that none is visible in a release, guidance with its bases and pregnancy flags, the red-flag boundary, a golden seed and two safety vignettes, and wording in both languages. The 23 original patterns do not meet every row — no pattern has a recorded textbook source, six have no quotation of their own, four need the tongue and pulse to be reported and their seeds hold none, the red-flag boundary was never recorded, only one pattern has the two vignettes — so each gap is **waived by name, with its reason**, in `data/review/admission.json`; a waiver can name only an original pattern and fails the build as soon as it is no longer needed, so the list can only shrink, and a new pattern is never waived. Declarations (needs the exam, no release formula, a margin exception) are checked against the scores and the computed tiers. For a *candidate*, `scripts/review/dossier.py` writes the page a clinical reviewer decides on before any weights exist — sources, overlap with every pattern measured with the engine's own scoring, the questions that are missing, the red flags nearby, the formulas, the cost against the budget — and `scripts/kb/new_pattern.py` writes the skeleton of everything an accepted candidate needs. The waves of content themselves (PM-22, PM-23) are not started: they need named reviewers.

### Herb browser (in progress, Release C)
- **The herb browser's data** (PM-24): the 703 herbs of the knowledge base are delivered for browsing without touching the session: a compact index (26.5 KB gzip: names, category, nature, flavours, channels, the first functions, flags, pregnancy level, status) and sixteen shards (3–5 KB each) that hold a herb's whole page — all functions, the Pharmacopoeia's caution text, the stored interaction flags, the classical formulas, the source. They are fetched only when someone browses herbs (a list asks for the index, a page for its one shard), checked against the hashes in the manifest, kept, and asked for again if a fetch failed; they are not part of the knowledge-base version (a change to a herb page marks no saved result as old) or of the 100 KB session figure (87.4 KB, unchanged), and each has its Simplified list. They carry **no dose, no herb weights and no repository path** in any profile. The closed beta and the dev build hold all herbs, each with its status; a public build lists only herbs a sample review has covered — none yet, so it has no herb file at all. The release check gains a rule for all of it (hashes, budgets, index and shards agreeing, nothing a page may not show, the review status, the Simplified lists). The pages themselves are the next task.

- **Herb pages** (PM-25): *Herbs* is a kind in Learn, in a build that has the herb browser (the closed beta and dev; a public build has none until a sample review has covered herbs). The list shows the 703 herbs by category in a textbook's order, each with what its record flags — *Toxic*, *Avoid in pregnancy*, *Pregnancy caution* — a filter by name (Chinese in either script, English, Latin, the address, a function) and a select for the nature. A herb page puts first the five things its record supports — toxic or no toxicity recorded, the pregnancy level, every stored interaction or none, the allergy line, the practitioner line — and the Pharmacopoeia's caution text as it is, in Chinese; then category, nature, flavours, channel tropism, functions, the formulas of the app that use the herb, the source and how far it has been checked, with a note on why the page is a draft. The source line is honest about three kinds of herb: the Pharmacopoeia (637), a textbook for herbs it does not list (63), and none for the three added by hand. A page opened by its address fetches one shard of 3–5 KB, never the index; a failed fetch says so and is tried again; the herb pages work offline after one visit. Found and fixed on the way: the three hand-added herbs had their functions filed under *caution* (a page would have shown 粳米's functions as a caution); the Learn lists said "1 entries shown"; and when a page arrives after the route, keyboard focus now moves to its heading (it stayed on the page's container). The sample review of the herbs is still a human task: no herb is `reviewed`, so every page says it is a draft. In the hub, search does not cover herbs — the list has its own filter.

### Seasons (in progress, Release C)
- **How seasons are counted** (PM-26): the season of a result is the one the person lives. *Settings → Seasons* offers the **northern calendar** (the default, as before), the **southern hemisphere** (the season is looked up at the Sun's longitude + 180°: spring begins in early August, summer in early November, autumn in early February, winter in early May) and **no seasons** for the tropics, where four seasons are not the climate. Until a choice is made the device's time zone suggests the northern or the southern calendar (a short list of southern zones; never *no seasons*), and the card says that it is only a suggestion; the app says in one sentence that it knows the calendar and the choice, not the climate where the person is. A result says *Season: Autumn (southern hemisphere)* beside a link to the choice, the coming seasons are listed in the order the person lives them (and are named in the page's language — the English page used to print them in Chinese), and a result made without seasons has no season block, no coming seasons and no susceptibility to a season. Only the season changes: the birth chart, the solar terms, the annual block and the year's five periods and six qi are calendar constructs and are the same everywhere, and under the other season model the days of 土 are the same days in both hemispheres. **A northern result is exactly what it was**: seven whole results, hashed from the unmodified engine before the change, are pinned by a test, and so are the northern season on every fifth day from 1900 to 2100 in both models and the stamp of the parameters; a result made on another basis carries its own stamp and starts a series of its own in the history (the trends never compare across a basis), and a saved result is proved genuine by making it again on the basis it was made on, so a backup of a southern result restores as genuine. The choice is a preference (low sensitivity) and travels in a backup when chosen. A link may now name a place on a page and focus goes there (`RouteFocus`), and a screen that was hidden while its code loaded no longer takes the focus. Draft; the clinical content owner still confirms what flips ([design §4.4](docs/post-mvp/design/five-phase-extensions.md#44-as-built-pm-26)).

- **The season model, declared** (PM-29): the school choice behind the season is no longer hidden. Every result with a season says in one line which reading of the year stands behind it — *late summer (長夏) is counted as a season of its own, between summer and autumn* — with a plain explanation one click away (schools divide the year in different ways; this app uses one reading and says so; the choice changes only the season block of the reference, never how symptoms are scored). The foot of the practitioner summary and the summary file say the same (two new **optional** fields of the file's `exportedFrom`; the schema stays version 1). A release declares the default model and has no switch; the development build has one in *Settings → Seasons* to try the other, and a result made with the other model ends its parameter stamp `+tuwang18`, so it starts a series of its own in the history and the default leaves no trace. The second model's days of 土 now have an English name (*Earth days*) instead of printing 土旺 on an English page. The school decision itself stays open until calibration ([design §7.1](docs/post-mvp/design/five-phase-extensions.md#71-as-built-pm-29)).

- **The astronomy tables, reproducible** (PM-28): the Earth series behind every solar term are regenerated from the official VSOP87D archive (CDS/VizieR, its SHA-256 pinned) by a script in the repository; every term came out exactly as before, so no date or season moves.

### Hours of birth (in progress, Release C)
- **A birth time near a change of hour** (PM-27): about a quarter of all birth times fall within 15 minutes of the change between two hours, where the hour pillar — and, at 23:00, the day pillar — could be the other side's. The five-phase module now measures that distance in true solar time (so a clock time far from a boundary can be near one, and the other way round) and names the pillars on the other side by the chart's own rule. On the birth card a time within the margin gets a question after the daylight-saving choice — *Your birth time is close to a change of hour … Which is nearer the truth?* — with three answers: **the computed hour** (the default, so nothing changes unless the person says so), **the other hour**, and **I am not sure — leave the hour out** (the existing unknown-hour path). It is not asked for an unknown hour or a time that is not near a change, and it is asked again when the date, the time, the place or the daylight-saving choice is edited. The answer is an input like the birth data itself: it is kept with the unfinished assessment only together with the birth data, and a saved result keeps **the choice and not the time** (the pillars and the true solar time are still removed unless the birth data is remembered) and says on the result which hour its birth chart was made from. It is not a parameter: the engine, the stamps and the fingerprint of a result made with the choice are those of one made without it, and a result made before it existed comes out of the engine again exactly as it was ([design §5.1](docs/post-mvp/design/five-phase-extensions.md#51-as-built-pm-27)). Draft; the margin and the default are for the usability round.

### File sync (in progress, Release D)
- **Keep a backup file up to date** (PM-32, Chromium only): in a browser that can choose a file to write, *Settings* has a card that keeps an **encrypted backup** current in a file the person chooses — in a folder their own cloud service keeps in step between their devices. After each change to the saved results (a few seconds later, while the app is open and the history is not locked) the app writes every result and the settings to it; the passphrase is typed twice to set it up, asked for in each later session and never stored; a passphrase typed later is checked against the file, so a slip cannot change the passphrase the other devices read. **A file that is not as this device left it is never overwritten**: the card says it holds changes this device does not have, and the person merges it with the restore dialog first (skip, keep both, replace if newer); a file that is not an encrypted backup of this app is refused and left alone. Every way a write can fail — the tab closed in the middle, no room, the file held by a cloud client, moved or deleted, the permission taken back — leaves the file as it was, is said in words, and is tried again on a click. Nothing goes to any server of this app; the cloud service sees only an encrypted file. Where the browser cannot choose a file (Safari, Firefox) there is no card, no row in what is stored and no word in Erase, and the manual backup and the share sheet remain. The spike's protocol was run as written and no stop condition was met ([research tracks §4.1](docs/post-mvp/design/research-tracks.md#41-as-built-pm-32)); the all-JavaScript budget is now 347 of 350 KB.

### Backup (in progress, Release A)
- **The backup engine** (PM-07): a versioned JSON file with a checksum, built from what the person chooses to include (their saved results; preferences — never the disclaimer acknowledgement; the unfinished assessment only if asked). The importer treats a file as untrusted: size and depth bounds, an allow-list validator that builds fresh plain objects (unknown keys dropped, wrong values refused with a reason, `__proto__` and its kin refused), a release build refusing what a development build made, and a replay check — a record made by this version must come out of the engine again exactly as written, so an altered result is rejected as not following from its answers without any secret. Records made by another version are kept as saved and marked *Imported*. The person's choice becomes one transaction: all of it or none. Round-trip, fuzz and atomicity tests; the lint configuration now refuses every way of rendering a string as markup. The screens follow (PM-08).

- **Back up and restore, on screen** (PM-08): Settings → *Your data* makes a backup — a list of what goes in (the saved results to untick from, the settings, the unfinished assessment if asked), a plain warning that the file holds health information and that where it is kept is the person's choice — and restores from a file: what the file holds, what is new, what is already here, what differs (keep what is here, keep both, or take the newer), what is left out and why, and then one change that happens whole or not at all. History marks results that came from another version *Imported*, and says on the result that it is shown as saved. A reminder appears on History and the start page when results are not in a backup (five of them, or thirty days; *Not now* waits two weeks; a switch in Settings turns it off), and a quiet line on History and at the end of a result opens the same dialog. In all three languages; end-to-end E24 restores a backup in a second browser to an equal history.

- **Passphrase-protected backups** (PM-09): a backup can be protected with a passphrase — ten characters typed twice, a hint about its strength, and a plain warning that a forgotten passphrase cannot be recovered by anyone. The file is compressed where the browser can and encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key made by PBKDF2-SHA-256 at 600 000 iterations; its header (format, time, compression, key derivation, cipher) is not secret but is authenticated, so changing any of it fails like a wrong passphrase, and it says nothing about what is inside, not even how many results. Restoring asks for the passphrase; a wrong one changes nothing and says one thing; a file that asks for absurd settings is refused before any work is done. Everything is WebCrypto — no dependency — and checked against published test vectors and Node's own implementation.

- **Storage health** (PM-10): Settings → Your data says how much room the app uses on this device, whether the browser has agreed to keep it, and — always, in plain words — why data can disappear (private windows, Safari's week, cleared browsing data, another browser or device). After the first saved result, and only when the browser has not agreed, a button asks it to keep the data; nothing is asked until it is pressed, and either answer is reported. A browser without the API shows the explanation alone; blocked storage says the data is not being saved. The short form is inside the backup dialog.

### Pulse

### Safety
- **A flavour-excess caution on 天王補心丹.** With 地黃 甘, as its Pharmacopoeia text says (see *Fixed*), 甘 carries 62 % of 天王補心丹 (51 % before) — over the 55 % of `R_FLAVOR_EXCESS` — so the soft caution now comes with the formula wherever it is recommended (the development profile; the release profile recommends no formula at its level). No other formula crosses the line.

- **No silent default region for the emergency numbers.** The MVP showed Taiwan's numbers to anyone who had not chosen a region, wherever they were. Numbers now appear for the region the person chose or whose time zone the device is in (named on screen, changeable); anyone else sees "call your local emergency number" and a request to choose. Each region row carries the time zones that preselect it and an optional dated verification record; a public build (no draft label) ships only verified rows no older than 24 months (`check-release` rule 12).

- **An allergy is matched whichever script it was typed in** (PM-33). The rule used to compare characters as they are, so in a Traditional session 人参 matched nothing and was reported as unconfirmed; it now folds the entry and the names to one script with a table generated from the names (`data/safety/name-fold.json`, 182 characters) and a vignette suite entry for each script. Found on the way: a formula's own name for a herb (芍藥, 龍膽草, 白豆蔻) was not among the names an allergy could match when the herb record says 白芍, 龍膽, 豆蔻; it is now.

### Fixed
- **Herb natures and flavours that disagreed with the text they cite.** The build copied 四氣 and 五味 from the reference library's structured summary of each entry, and in 11 of the 700 entries the summary disagrees with the 2025 Pharmacopoeia text in the same file: 生薑 溫 (the text: 微溫), 菟絲子 溫 (平), 白蘞, 野菊花 and 槓板歸 寒 (微寒), 水紅花子 涼 (微寒), 黃蜀葵花 and 溪黃草 涼 (寒); 虎杖 苦 (微苦), 炮薑 辛、苦 (辛), and 地黃 甘、苦 where its 生地黃 statement says 甘 (the 苦 is 鮮地黃's). The records now take the text (`curated/herbs.py` `SIQI_CORRECTIONS` and `WUWEI_CORRECTIONS`, each noted in the record's `data_quality`), and a test checks every herb's 四氣 and 五味 against the 性味 of its source (`scripts/kb/tests/test_herb_xingwei.py`, in `pnpm test:kb`). The herb pages say so, and the food 生薑 is 微溫 ("slightly warm"); two names now have the Traditional 薑, 炮薑 and 薑半夏 (were 炮姜, 姜半夏). No formula's panel effect or tier changes and the golden report is the same. What moves: the derived 陰陽 of the herbs (生薑 0.57 → 0.48, 菟絲子 0.48 → 0.30); the burden of the five formulas with 地黃 is lighter (脾.qi of 益胃湯 −0.435 → −0.339, 天王補心丹 −0.335 → −0.25; 腎氣丸, 龍膽瀉肝湯, 血府逐瘀湯) and their share of 苦 smaller, so they fit a little better — 益胃湯 passes 麥門冬湯 for the typical 肺陰虛 patient (second and third), golden case G-0023 no longer adds 粳米 in its development-profile 加減, and in `docs/formula-verification.md` 天王補心丹 now ranks third for its own pattern (indication findings 8 → 7) and 桂枝湯's 宣 is +0.35 (was +0.36); the personalised prescription no longer lowers 生薑 in summer (用熱遠熱) nor 菟絲子 in spring and summer, and for the typical 血瘀 patient 血府逐瘀湯 adds 白扁豆 instead of 白朮 and 烏梅. Found while writing the course (PM-55).
- Two tests that lost a race under a full run now wait for what they act on: the backup dialog's list before *Download*, and the stored dismissal before the follow-up test leaves the page.
